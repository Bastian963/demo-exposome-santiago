# Public transport accessibility layer (OSM bus + Metro)

Commune-level public transport accessibility for the Región
Metropolitana de Santiago, derived from **OpenStreetMap** bus stops
and Metro S.A. stations via `osmnx`. The layer summarizes the
**physical availability** of public transport (counts, density,
distance, coverage) — not service frequency, headway reliability, or
fare affordability.

## Why include public transport

Access to public transport is a structural determinant of social
participation, healthcare access, and economic opportunity, and is
increasingly recognized as a **modifiable dementia risk factor**
through the activity-and-social-contact pathway (Lancet Commission
on dementia prevention, 2024 update: social isolation contributes
~4% of modifiable dementia risk). The layer complements
`healthcare_access` (which measures facility proximity) by providing
the broader mobility infrastructure that determines whether
residents can reach those facilities.

## Regional reference values

- **Santiago Metro**: 7 lines (L1, L2, L3, L4, L4a, L5, L6), 137
  stations as of 2024, operated by Metro S.A.
- **EFE/MetroTren**: 4 commuter rail lines (Ramal, Tren Central,
  Bus-BioBio, Arica-La Paz, Alameda-Nos), ~80 stations. **Not
  included in the index** (informative only) because the layer
  focuses on intracity urban mobility.
- **RED bus system**: integrated by DTPM, ~11 815 unique stops
  after deduplication in this layer.
- **DTPM service quality standards** (2023): 80% of population
  within 500 m of a bus stop is the design target for the urban
  perimeter.

`run_public_transport.py` uses `osmnx.features_from_polygon` to
download all bus stops, Metro stations, and rail stations within
each commune polygon, deduplicates by spatial proximity (snap to
nearest existing stop within 25 m), and computes density, distance,
and 300/500/1000 m coverage metrics using a regular 100 m grid
sampling per commune. `plot_public_transport_map.py` produces a
4-panel figure (chloropleth of the transit index, bus density,
ranked bar chart, NSE Spearman correlation).

## Metrics

All 52 communes × 14 columns. Columns:

- **`name`** — commune name (UTF-8, INE 2017 spelling).
- **`transit_n_bus_stops`** — unique bus stops after deduplication.
  Range: 7 (Alhué) to 558 (Santiago).
- **`transit_n_metro_stations`** — Metro S.A. stations in commune.
  Range: 0 (rural) to 21 (Santiago).
- **`transit_n_rail_stations`** — EFE/MetroTren stations (informative
  only, not in index). Range: 0 to 3 (Buin).
- **`transit_bus_density`** — bus stops per km². Range: 0 to ~7.
- **`transit_metro_density`** — Metro stations per km². Range: 0 to
  ~1.5.
- **`transit_mean_dist_bus_m`** — mean distance (m) from commune
  interior grid points to nearest bus stop. Range: 742 (dense
  urban) to 7 425 (Alhué).
- **`transit_p90_dist_bus_m`** — 90th percentile distance (m) to
  nearest bus stop. Captures tail of underserved areas.
- **`transit_bus_coverage_300m`** — share of commune interior within
  300 m of a bus stop. Range: 0 to 1.0.
- **`transit_bus_coverage_500m`** — share of commune interior within
  500 m of a bus stop. Range: 0 to 1.0.
- **`transit_mean_dist_metro_m`** — mean distance (m) to nearest
  Metro station. 50 000 for communes with no Metro.
- **`transit_metro_coverage_1000m`** — share of commune interior
  within 1 000 m of a Metro station. Range: 0 to 1.0.
- **`transit_has_metro`** — binary (1 = at least one Metro station
  in commune). 27 of 52 communes have Metro.
- **`transit_index`** — composite 0-100 score (weighted blend of
  bus stop density, bus 500m coverage, Metro 1km coverage, with
  Metro presence doubling the score). Used as the master ranking
  metric. Range: 0 (San José de Maipo) to 100 (Santiago); 27
  communes have Metro presence.

### Top 5 / bottom 5 by `transit_index`

- **Top 5** (best-served): Santiago 100.0, Lo Prado 96.9, Providencia
  90.4, Ñuñoa 87.8, San Miguel 87.4.
- **Bottom 5** (worst-served): San José de Maipo 0.0, Lo Barnechea
  19.1, Alhué 23.5, Curacaví 24.5, Colina 25.3.

## Interpretation

The top of the distribution follows the **Metro L1 corridor**:
Santiago (L1+L2+L3+L5), Providencia (L1), Ñuñoa (L3+L6), San Miguel
(L2). Lo Prado's high score (96.9) is a positive outlier — it sits
at the L5 western terminus and benefits from terminal station
density. The bottom is dominated by **periurban/rural communes**
with no Metro access and sparse bus coverage: San José de Maipo
(only 54 bus stops across ~5 000 km², the largest commune in RM
by area), Lo Barnechea (low bus density despite high NSE; the
commune is large and the eastern Andean valleys are bus-sparse),
Alhué, Curacaví, Colina.

The `transit_index` is **positively** correlated with NSE within the
urban perimeter (Spearman ρ ≈ +0.55 in this layer) but **inversely**
correlated with commune area in the rural subset. Users should
interpret the index in conjunction with `demography.pop_density` to
distinguish rural sparsity from urban inequality.

`transit_metro_coverage_1000m=1.0` for Santiago (L1 corridor) and Lo
Prado (L5 terminal) is a **structural finding**: the communes that
contain Metro terminals achieve full interior coverage of the
1 000 m catchment. Santiago additionally contains the L2/L3/L5
transfer hubs (Universidad de Chile, Plaza de Armas, Santa Ana) that
densify coverage to the entire commune interior.

## Brain-health / exposome interpretation

- **Healthcare access pathway**: residents of low-`transit_index`
  communes face longer travel times to specialists → deferred
  diagnosis of dementia precursors (cognitive evaluation, vascular
  risk management). This is a **mechanism** linking public transport
  to dementia-relevant outcomes.
- **Social isolation pathway**: low transit access reduces visits
  to family, religious services, and community centres. The
  *Lancet Commission 2024* lists social isolation as a modifiable
  risk factor (~4% of attributable dementia risk).
- **Air pollution / active mobility pathway**: dense public
  transport in central communes co-occurs with high PM2.5 and
  noise exposure → public transport is a *necessary but not
  sufficient* condition for healthy urban mobility. Layer should
  be modelled jointly with `air_quality` and `noise`.
- **Equity**: the bottom 5 are **mostly rural**, not poor urban
  areas. Rural transit poverty is a different policy problem from
  urban transit poverty; the layer captures both.

## Limitations

1. **OSM coverage**: bus stop completeness in OSM varies; some
   communes (San José de Maipo, Pirque) may have un-mapped rural
   stops. Cross-validation against DTPM's official stop registry
   (RED) is a future improvement.
2. **No service frequency**: a stop with 1 bus/hour is treated
   equal to a stop with 30 buses/hour. The layer captures
   *availability*, not *service quality*.
3. **No headway / reliability**: scheduled vs actual headway not
   measured. Metro is generally high-frequency; buses vary.
4. **No fare affordability**: BIP! card integration assumed; cash
   fares not separately modelled.
5. **No pedestrian access to stops**: the layer does not measure
   whether the *path* to the bus stop is itself walkable (covered
   by `walkability` layer separately).
6. **Metro-only for metro metric**: only Metro S.A. stations count
   toward `transit_n_metro_stations` and `transit_index`. EFE/
   MetroTren stations are reported in `transit_n_rail_stations`
   but excluded from the index (the index is a measure of urban
   mobility, not regional rail).
7. **Coverage metric definition**: coverage is computed against a
   uniform 100 m grid sampling of the commune polygon, weighted
   equally per grid point (not per capita). Low-density communes
   (San José de Maipo) are penalized by their area, not by their
   population.

## Reproducibility

- Source: OpenStreetMap via `osmnx.features_from_polygon`.
- Tags: `highway=bus_stop` (bus), `railway=station/halt/subway_entrance`
  filtered post-download to Metro S.A. (rail); `railway=station/halt`
  (EFE/MetroTren, informative only).
- Deduplication: spatial snap to nearest existing stop within 25 m.
- Coverage: 100 m grid sampling per commune polygon, 300/500/1 000 m
  buffers.
- CRS metric: `EPSG:32719` (UTM 19S, Chile-specific).
- Re-run: `python scripts/run_public_transport.py` (requires
  network; cache per-commune under `cache/public_transport_*` if
  needed).
- Random seeds: none.
- Pre-processed output: deterministic given same OSM snapshot date.
- Cache: optional, per commune.
