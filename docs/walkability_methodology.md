# Walkability layer (OSM street-network metrics)

Commune-level street-network walkability metrics for the Región
Metropolitana de Santiago, derived from **OpenStreetMap** street
geometry via `osmnx`. The layer summarizes the **physical
configurability** of the pedestrian network (intersection density,
block length, circuity) — not a direct estimate of walk trips, walk
mode share, or pedestrian perception.

## Why include walkability

Walkable urban form is associated with higher physical activity, lower
obesity, better cardiometabolic health, and lower social isolation in
older adults. For the BrainLat exposome, walkability is a
**behavioural pathway** that mediates the relationship between the
built environment and dementia-relevant outcomes (cognitive reserve
via active mobility; social contact via street-level encounters; see
*The Lancet Commission on dementia prevention, 2024 update*).

## Regional reference values

- **Sane walkability** in dense Latin-American cities:
  - Intersection density: 100-200 int/km² is typical of mixed-use
    urban fabric (Ewing & Cervero 2010 meta-analysis).
  - Average block length: 100-150 m is walkable; > 250 m is
    car-oriented.
  - Circuity (network ratio actual/straight-line): 1.0-1.2 = grid;
    > 1.4 = dendritic / cul-de-sac.
- **Santiago-specific**: OSM coverage of RM is mature for the urban
  core (Santiago, Providencia, Las Condes, Ñuñoa) and progressively
  sparser in periurban communes. The layer uses `network_type='all'`
  (drive + walk + service) to capture pedestrian-accessible streets.

(intersection density, street density, average street length,

the walk index, intersection density, ranked bar chart, NSE
Spearman correlation).

## Metrics

All 52 communes × 8 columns. Columns:

- **`name`** — commune name (UTF-8, INE 2017 spelling).
- **`walk_intersection_density`** — intersections per km² of commune
  area. Range: 0.27 (San José de Maipo) to 578.6 (Ñuñoa).
- **`walk_street_density_km_km2`** — total street length (km) per km².
  Range: 399 to 43 692.
- **`walk_avg_street_length_m`** — average block length in metres.
  Range: 39 (Ñuñoa) to 501 (San José de Maipo).
- **`walk_streets_per_node`** — connectivity ratio (streets/intersection
  node). 3.0 = fully grid; < 2.5 = dendritic.
- **`walk_circuity`** — actual/straight-line distance ratio. 1.0 =
  perfect grid; > 1.4 = winding.
- **`walk_n_nodes`** — total intersection nodes in commune network.
  Range: 687 (Alhué) to 8 576 (Buin — high count due to area, not
  density).
- **`walk_index`** — composite 0-100 score (percentile-rank
  re-scaling of intersection density + inverse of block length),
  used as the master ranking metric. Range: 0 (San José de Maipo) to
  100 (La Granja); mean 59.8.

### Top 5 / bottom 5 by `walk_index`

- **Top 5** (most walkable, dense central/inner-ring): La Granja
  100.0, Ñuñoa 96.2, Providencia 94.8, San Joaquín 91.2, San Ramón
  89.7.
- **Bottom 5** (rural, sparse): San José de Maipo 0.0, San Pedro
  15.9, Alhué 16.0, Tiltil 19.3, Curacaví 22.9.

## Interpretation

Walkability peaks in the **inner-ring communes** that experienced
heavy 20th-century urbanization with regular grids: La Granja, San
Joaquín, San Ramón, Ñuñoa. These communes have short blocks
(40-60 m), high intersection density (400-580 int/km²), and grid
circuity near 1.0. The pattern is **not** identical to NSE:
La Granja, San Joaquín and San Ramón are middle-to-low NSE yet
top-quintile walkable — they combine dense pedestrian fabric with
modest commercial activity.

The bottom of the distribution is dominated by **rural and
periurban communes** (San José de Maipo, San Pedro, Alhué, Tiltil,
Curacaví) where OSM coverage is sparse and street geometry is
dominated by long rural roads. `walk_index=0` for San José de Maipo
is a **structural sparsity**, not a true zero — it reflects the OSM
network at commune-scale, where 1 pixel = 1km captures only the
central town grid.

`walk_circuity` is **inversely** related to block size: short-block
grids have circuity 1.05-1.15, while dendritic rural networks
exceed 1.5 (range observed 1.05-1.85 in this layer).

## Brain-health / exposome interpretation

- **Active mobility**: short blocks + high intersection density
  correlate with higher walking trips (Ewing & Cervero 2010
  meta-analysis: 0.3-0.5 SD shift per quartile of intersection
  density). Walking contributes to cardiovascular and cognitive
  reserve.
- **Social contact**: dense, fine-grained networks support
  spontaneous street-level encounters; relevant for loneliness in
  older adults (a modifiable dementia risk factor per *Lancet
  Commission 2024*).
- **Air pollution co-exposure**: walkable central communes (Santiago,
  Providencia, Ñuñoa) are also high PM2.5 + noise exposure →
  walkability × air pollution interaction should be modelled, not
  treated as independent.
- **Equity**: walkability is **not** synonymous with NSE; some
  middle-NSE communes (San Joaquín, San Ramón) rank top-quintile
  walkable. Equity analysis should not assume "walkable = rich".

## Limitations

1. **OSM coverage bias**: rural communes have sparse OSM coverage;
   the layer under-counts their true network. Mitigation: combine
   with INE cartography for full-coverage comparisons (not done in
   this layer; flagged as a future improvement).
2. **Network type**: `network_type='all'` includes service roads and
   pedestrian alleys; pure pedestrian-only paths are not counted
   separately.
3. **No amenities**: this layer measures **physical configuration
   only**. Land-use mix, retail density, sidewalk quality, and
   perceived safety are not captured (those belong to separate
   layers, e.g. `social_infrastructure`).
4. **No slope / topography**: Santiago's eastern communes have steep
   terrain (Vitacura, Lo Barnechea, San José de Maipo) that
   physically limits walking; the layer does not penalize slope.
5. **Walk index formula**: composite of two metrics (intersection
   density + inverse block length) rescaled to 0-100. Not a
   validated walkability index in the published literature
   (e.g. Walk Score uses a proprietary formula); suitable for
   **relative ranking**, not absolute comparison.
6. **CRS metric**: all network metrics computed in `EPSG:32719`
   (UTM 19S, Chile-specific) to get accurate km/km² units. Cannot
   be directly compared to walkability layers in other cities
   without re-projection.

## Reproducibility

- Source: OpenStreetMap via `osmnx` (`osmnx.graph_from_place`).
- Network type: `all` (drive + walk + service).
- Buffer: 0 m (commune polygon as-is).
- CRS metric: `EPSG:32719` (UTM 19S, Chile-specific).
- Re-run: `python scripts/run_walkability.py` (requires network
  access; cache per-commune graphs under `cache/walkability_*` if
  needed).
- Random seeds: none.
- Cache: optional, per commune; safe to interrupt and resume.
- Pre-processed output: `data/processed/santiago_walkability.csv`
  is deterministic given the same OSM snapshot date.
